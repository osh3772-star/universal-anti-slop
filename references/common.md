# Common audit — all deliverables

For each finding, point to a concrete passage/slide/element/file. Do not label an output 'AI slop' without evidence.

## Check for
- **Generic defaults:** a category-standard output that ignores the specific user, audience, product, or setting.
- **Empty claims:** 'innovative', 'premium', 'revolutionary', 'seamless' without an observable feature, example, or data.
- **Unnecessary repetition:** duplicate headings, cards, ideas, visual treatment, or restated conclusions.
- **Mechanical symmetry:** every paragraph, slide, section or card forced into the same pattern regardless of purpose.
- **Decoration without function:** gratuitous gradients, glow, shadows, icons, motion, bold text, or transitions.
- **Missing hierarchy:** everything looks equally important; actions, conclusions, and evidence cannot be found quickly.
- **Fake specificity:** invented citations, fabricated experiences, unjustified percentages, decorative dashboards with fake metrics.
- **Over-engineering:** a large framework or many moving parts for a problem that needs a small, clear solution.
- **Tone mismatch:** over-friendliness, grandiosity, performative certainty, or humor not wanted by the audience.
- **Sterile overcorrection:** removing all texture, personality, emotion, color, or helpful structure just to be 'minimal'.

## Safe revision pattern
Observe -> state why it harms the work -> propose a smaller or more specific replacement -> change only with proper authority -> recheck.

## Quality evidence
- A reader can name the central decision or insight.
- Text and visual forms serve the specific task, not a template.
- Important claims have evidence or are identified as interpretation.
- The work retains intentional brand/personal style.
- There is no demonstrated functional regression.

## Never
- Treat any single word, color, or layout as proof AI created it.
- Deliberately inject spelling errors, awkward grammar, misaligned elements, or random novelty.
- Remove required accessibility features, citations, disclaimers, or safety labels.
- Claim an automated detector can reliably establish authorship.
