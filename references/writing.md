# Writing / scripts / translations / UI copy

## Detect
- Empty scene-setting introductions and rephrased summaries that add no information.
- Repeated contrast formulas ('not just A but B'), synthetic epiphanies, forced three-part lists, and mechanical rhythmic punctuation.
- Filler praise and adjectives with no referent.
- Every sentence made equally short/long or every paragraph the same length.
- Generic voice that overrides the author's own phrasing; repetitive transition phrases.
- Corporate jargon, false claims of certainty, invented quotes/sources, unsupported examples.
- Markdown/bolding applied as decoration in prose intended to be read naturally.

## Revise
- Lead with the actual point where appropriate. Name concrete actions, entities, conditions and examples.
- Vary rhythm only when it helps meaning. Remove redundancy and keep purposeful repetition.
- Preserve genre and voice: formal reports remain formal, fiction retains texture, personal writing retains idiosyncrasies.
- For Korean: prioritize clear subjects, concrete verbs and short direct sentences; do not make all prose telegraphic.
- For Japanese: preserve register (敬語/常体), nuance, names, particles, and provided original text; distinguish translation edits from stylistic edits.
- Do not 'humanize' by inventing emotions, personal anecdotes or unverifiable quotations.

## Verify
Meaning unchanged; numbers/quotes/citations correct; speaker and addressee stable; key action obvious; no unexplained deletion of a meaningful sentence.

## Example (Korean)
Before: '오늘날 빠르게 변화하는 디지털 환경에서 혁신적인 솔루션은 그 어느 때보다 중요합니다.'
After: '기존 매물 입력은 같은 정보를 세 번 입력해야 한다. 자동화하면 중복 입력을 줄일 수 있다.'
The after example is appropriate only where the real workflow supports the stated fact; otherwise use a verified example.
